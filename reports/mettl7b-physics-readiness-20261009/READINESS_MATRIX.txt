A. RELATIVE BINDING FREE ENERGY: EXACT QUESTION AND MODEL
Question: within a specified receptor/SAM/ligand protonation ensemble, does
conversion of parent to AR make binding less favorable? Define
DDG = DG_bind(AR) - DG_bind(parent); positive favors parent within that model.
Do not derive a target DDG from23.04uM IC50 and the qualitative AR panel.

Use a two-environment thermodynamic cycle: transform parent->AR in solvent and
in the bound complex. Same-charge transformations are possible for matched
neutral or amine-protonated endpoints; ester->alcohol changes atom types, H
inventory and partial charges, not only deletion of ten heavy atoms. Soft-core
nonbonded treatment, bonded/dummy terms, restraints and electrostatic conventions
must be explicit and independently checked. Net-charge-changing cycles require
additional finite-size/protonation treatment; do not mix them silently.

The large removed cap and alternate predicted sites raise sampling risks, not a
categorical ban on RBFE. A staged alchemical path can improve overlap but cannot
repair a wrong binding site. Disagreeing basin-conditioned answers cannot be
averaged by docking frequency. Absolute double-decoupling per admitted basin
is an alternative if common-mode RBFE is unsuitable, but needs standard-state
restraint corrections and basin/population accounting and is more costly.
MM/GBSA or MM/PBSA could be a model-dependent diagnostic after sampling; neither
is a justified shortcut to a definitive parent/AR binding free energy here.

Before production: endpoint Hamiltonians must reproduce independently loaded
energies/forces, identity and charge; solvent and bound endpoint ensembles must
be stable; test both transformation directions, lambda overlap, equilibration,
correlation and independent starts. Freeze the estimator and protocol version.
Report statistical uncertainty separately from force-field/pose/state uncertainty.
An apparently small error bar with trapped modes is not convergence.
No target-specific numerical convergence threshold is fabricated in this report;
freeze it with the pilot's measured diagnostics before expensive production.

C. CATALYTIC MODELING: EXACT QUESTION AND MODEL
Question: for an explicitly admitted reactive state, does the inhibitor change
the methyl-transfer activation free energy, rather than merely bind differently?
The base reaction is an SN2-like S_nucleophile--C_methyl--S_SAM transformation.
An initial reaction-coordinate candidate is the difference of forming and
breaking C--S distances. Neutral RSH additionally requires an explicit proton
acceptor and proton-transfer coordinate; pre-existing RS- is a distinct chemical
state, not a free assumption. Both branches need independent chemical evaluation.

Do not assign C203 or a nearby residue as general base from proximity. Compare
reactant/product electronic states and plausible proton routes, verify spin and
charge, and test the chosen electronic method on a small sulfur/sulfonium reaction
model before using it in the enzyme. Optimize/check stationary points or sample
a free-energy path as appropriate; a constrained energy maximum is not by itself
a validated transition state. Static potential-energy barriers are not activation
free energies or predicted rates.

For eventual embedding, include both reactive sulfur centers and the transferred
methyl in the QM region, plus any participating proton relay. Avoid cutting a
reactive/conjugated unit; test larger regions and charge-boundary sensitivity.
Preserve protein, solvent and long-range electrostatics; validate energies and
forces across the selected interface. An independently equilibrated ensemble,
window overlap and replicas are required for barrier differences. A small fragment
reaction benchmark is computationally resolvable and does not require adopting
the failed TSL force field, but it cannot establish the enzyme's proton route.
Putting all TSL in QM avoids using its MM intramolecular torsions in that region;
it does not automatically supply a valid pre-equilibrated ensemble or interface.
