# METTL7 recognition-shell literature integration

## Integrated conclusion

The evidence supports a conserved SAM-dependent catalytic center combined with
distributed, chemotype-dependent substrate recognition, but does not establish
that one METTL7A active site directly catalyzes thiol S-, guanine N7-, and
adenine N6-methylation.

The established thiol chemistry and the preparation-dependent m7G result are
compatible with catalytic promiscuity, but compatibility is not proof of a
single polypeptide mechanism. Direct m6A catalysis remains the weakest member of
the proposed triad.

The repository recognition sectors `39-47`, `144-151`, `195-203`, and `228-237`
form a plausible distributed shell around the SAM-centered pocket. Experimental
DCMB mutations implicate F43/Y47/F199 in ligand-state selection, but no published
RNA study tests those residues. The RNA-active 76-172 deletion interval overlaps
sector 144-151, yet that broad truncation cannot assign RNA recognition or
catalysis to the sector.

RNA need not fit wholly inside the small-molecule pocket. A defensible model is
an extended RNA-contact surface positioning one nucleotide near SAM while the
rest of the transcript remains solvent exposed. No current experiment maps that
surface or establishes the docking pose.

## Chemical comparison

- Thiolate S is already nucleophilic and can perform direct SN2 attack on the
  SAM methyl group when desolvated and geometrically aligned.
- Guanine N7 is a neutral-ring nucleophile; m7G formation does not require
  deprotonation of N7 but requires base orientation and electrostatic
  stabilization of the cationic product.
- Adenine N6 methylation generally requires control of exocyclic-amine geometry
  and proton transfer. It is the least straightforward chemistry to unify with
  thiolate methylation without an identified general base or partner.

## Fragment-design boundary

Athena's PLIP-like profiles localized reproducible METTL7B recognition around
196/203/205/206/207/210/211. They also showed METTL7A rerouting through
99/126/151/197/200. The outer methyl-to-ethyl edit perturbs an A-favorable state
while retaining the B network, but no B-tolerated feature that reliably causes
A rejection was established.
